## What the lines show

- SteamVR's standby event reaches a background application on the Frame.
  Both events fall within 3 ms of the XRService line that E100's journal
  showed.
- Each standby came 5.03 s after a `user_interaction_ended` (5.032 and
  5.031 s). The first removal shows `user_interaction_ended` at 25.2 s, the
  count the owner was asked to remove the headset at, interaction resuming
  1.5 s later and ending again at 32.9 s, so the first standby came 12.6 s
  after the first idle line and 5.0 s after the last.
- `proximity_on` arrived at each return, 41 and 83 ms after the standby
  ended. No `proximity_off` line and no `hmd_activity_changed` line was ever
  written; the activity level appears only on the other lines.

## Limits

A device report relayed by a peer session, recorded as received. It shows
that the watcher of #100 builds and runs on the Frame, joins SteamVR as a
background application without a camera, an overlay or a setting of its own,
receives the standby event, and ends on its bound. It is not a pass or fail
of row G7: no camera ran, no removal was timed from a click, and the owner
gave no wall-clock time for either removal, so the time from the headset
leaving the face to standby is known only against a spoken count. Both
standby periods were removals by the owner's account, so the run does not
show whether a worn, still headset can go idle and then to standby, which
matters for any armed run that asks the wearer to keep still. Why no
`proximity_off` was written is not known. SteamVR enabled legacy input for
the watcher's generated app key; nothing else in SteamVR's state is known to
have changed because of it. The run was on SteamOS 0.5.9 with 0.5.10 staged.
Every gate in `sightcircuit/gates.py` stays closed.
